Fireworks

Fireworks sends rockets up from the host's bottom edge that burst into a sphere of streaking sparks, which slow, fall, crackle and fade. Use it for a launch, a win or a celebration banner, or keep a show going behind a hero section. Shots are fired from code or on a press, or every so many seconds with Interval. They draw over the host and overlap.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add fireworks

Here a rocket goes up every 1.3 seconds over a night sky, and a press sends one more to the press point:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="10" Cursor="Hand">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Starfield Density="0.5" Twinkle="0.3" Speed="0.1" />
            <fx:Fireworks Interval="1.3" FireOnPress="True" Count="80" />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Border Width="360" Height="220" />
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

The starfield is a fill, drawn under the child as its background, so the child here is an empty Border that only sets the size.

Fire from code or on a press

Fire(at) sends a rocket to burst at a point in the host's coordinates. Without a point, each shot picks a spot of its own in the upper half of the shape. With FireOnPress, a press on the host fires at the press point.

private void OnLaunchClick(object? sender, RoutedEventArgs e)
{
    // Three rockets across the banner.
    var w = BannerHost.Bounds.Width;
    foreach (var x in new[] { 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 })
        BannerFireworks.Fire(new Point(w * x, 50));
}

Fired shots overlap: up to six play at once, and one more drops the oldest.

Keep a show going

Interval is the time in seconds between shots of a running show. At 0, the default, rockets go up only when fired. A show keeps the layer drawing every frame, so set Interval back to 0, or IsActive to false, when the show is over.

<fx:Fireworks x:Name="Show" Interval="0.8" Flight="0.9" Colors="#ffd23f, #ff4d6d, #4cc9f0" />

Shape the bursts

Count is the number of sparks a burst, Power how fast they leave, Gravity how fast they fall and Size the thickness of their streaks. Duration is how long a burst lasts and Flight how long a rocket climbs (0: the burst appears at once). Each burst takes one or two of the Colors.

Methods

Call Fire to send a rocket from code.

Method Description
Fire(Point? at = null) Sends a rocket to burst at at (host coordinates), or at a spot of its own.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Colors FXColorList Unset Burst colors; each burst takes one or two. Unset: a bright palette.
Count int 90 Sparks per burst. Range 8–240.
Power double 1 Burst speed, as a multiple of a lively default. Range 0.2–3.
Gravity double 1 How strongly the sparks fall after a burst, a multiple of the default (0: they don't fall). Range 0–3.
Duration double 1.6 How long a burst lasts. Range 0.4–4 seconds.
Flight double 0.7 How long a rocket climbs. Range 0–2 seconds.
Size double 2 Thickness of a spark's streak, DIPs. Range 0.5–6.
Interval double 0 Seconds between shots of a running show (0: only when fired). Range 0–10.
FireOnPress bool false Fires a rocket wherever the pointer is pressed on the host.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. While IsActive is off, presses fire nothing.

Notes

  • Light and dark. On a dark surface the sparks add light; on a light one they show as color. A BlendMode other than Normal set on the layer takes over. See Light and dark surfaces.
  • Reduced motion. There is no rocket: each burst appears in place and fades, without flying. See the poses.
  • Cost. The sparks are drawn on the GPU as one batch of soft strips, 0.04–0.18 ms of CPU time a frame on the desktop. Without a GPU they are drawn as stroked lines. Between shots, with no Interval, the layer draws nothing. RenderScale doesn't apply. See What a layer costs.
  • Room to burst. Bursts happen anywhere in the shape, and their sparks reach past it and fall. A clipping ancestor cuts them, so leave room around the host, or fire at points away from its edges.
