Water

Water puts the content under a sheet of water: moving the pointer over it drags ripples across the surface, a press drops a splash, and Rain lets drops fall on their own. The waves bend what is under them and catch the light. Use it for a playful hero, a calm "drop files here" area, or a pool on a game screen. A small wave simulation runs only while the water moves, and calm water draws nothing. It draws over the child, so it bends the child and whatever shows through it.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add water

Wrap the content in a host. Move the pointer over it for ripples, and press for a splash:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="14">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Water />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Border Width="260" Height="150" CornerRadius="14">
            <Border.Background>
                <RadialGradientBrush>
                    <GradientStop Color="#22D3EE" Offset="0" />
                    <GradientStop Color="#0E7490" Offset="1" />
                </RadialGradientBrush>
            </Border.Background>
            <TextBlock Text="Touch the water" Foreground="White" FontWeight="Bold" FontSize="20"
                       HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center" />
        </Border>
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

Give it something to bend

The water copies what is under it just before it draws (see Layers that read the backdrop): the child, and whatever shows through it. Text, edges and pictures show the waves best. A press splashes only when it lands on the host's child, so give a see-through child a Background, even Transparent. Inside an offscreen layer (an ancestor with Opacity below 1, an effect, or a clip that needs a layer) the copy can't see that ancestor's content, so keep such ancestors opaque.

Splash from code

Splash(at) drops a splash at a point in the host's coordinates, or at the center with no point. Set SplashOnPress="False" to stop presses from splashing:

// A splash where a drag ends over the pool.
private void OnPoolReleased(object? sender, PointerReleasedEventArgs e)
{
    PoolWater.Splash(e.GetPosition(PoolHost));
}

// A splash in the middle when a task completes.
PoolWater.Splash();

Let it rain

Rain is how many drops fall a second on their own; Seed places them. With rain, the water keeps moving and keeps drawing:

<fx:Water Rain="1.5" />

Tune the surface

Damping sets how long ripples last, Refraction how strongly the waves bend what is under them (1 is natural water), Stir how hard the pointer pushes, and Highlight the light on the waves. Tint colors the water where it rises, and its alpha is how much. CellSize is the simulation's cell size: smaller is finer and costs more.

<fx:Water Damping="0.992" Refraction="1.5" Stir="1.4" Tint="#4022D3EE" />

Methods

Call Splash to make waves without a press.

Method Description
Splash(Point? at = null) Drops a splash at at (host coordinates), or the center.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Damping double 0.985 How long ripples last: each 60th of a second keeps this share of the wave. Range 0.9–0.999.
Refraction double 1 How strongly the waves bend what is under them, a multiple: 1 is natural water, 0 none. Range 0–3.
Stir double 1 How hard the pointer pushes the water. Range 0–3.
Rain double 0 Drops a second falling on their own. Range 0–20 per second.
CellSize double 4 Simulation cell size: smaller is finer and costs more. Range 2–12 DIPs.
Highlight double 1 Highlights on the waves. Range 0–2.
Tint Color #303AA0FF A color where the water rises; its alpha is how much.
SplashOnPress bool true Drops a splash where the host is pressed.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale.

Notes

  • The pointer: ripples follow it while it is over the shape. See Layers that follow the pointer and One-shots.
  • Light and dark surfaces: it draws the same on both.
  • Reduced motion: gentler ripples and splashes, and no rain.
  • Cost: about 0.13 ms of GPU time a frame on the benchmark card while the water moves. The simulation steps at 60 Hz on the render thread; once the surface is flat it clears and stops, so calm water costs nothing. See Performance.
