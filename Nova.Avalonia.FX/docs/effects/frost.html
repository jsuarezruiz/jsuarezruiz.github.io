Frost

Frost grows ice in from the shape's edges in feathery crystal branches, frosting what is under it: blurred, whitened toward Tint and dotted with glints. Amount is how far the ice has crept in, so a transition on it freezes or thaws the content. It melts in a ragged circle around the pointer and freezes back when the pointer leaves. Use it for a locked or paused state, a "cold" plan in a pricing table, or a wintry promo. It draws over the child, so it frosts the child and whatever shows through it.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add frost

Wrap the content in a host. A picture or colorful content shows the frost best:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="14">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Frost Amount="0.72" />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Border Width="250" Height="150" CornerRadius="14">
            <Border.Background>
                <LinearGradientBrush StartPoint="0%,0%" EndPoint="100%,100%">
                    <GradientStop Color="#1E3A8A" Offset="0" />
                    <GradientStop Color="#7C3AED" Offset="0.55" />
                    <GradientStop Color="#F59E0B" Offset="1" />
                </LinearGradientBrush>
            </Border.Background>
            <TextBlock Text="Cold open" Foreground="White" FontWeight="Bold" FontSize="22"
                       HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center" />
        </Border>
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

Freeze and thaw

Amount runs from 0 (no ice, and nothing drawn) to 1 (covered). Give it a transition, and every change glides:

<Style Selector="fx|Frost">
    <Setter Property="Transitions">
        <Transitions>
            <DoubleTransition Property="Amount" Duration="0:0:1.2" />
        </Transitions>
    </Setter>
</Style>

CardFrost.Amount = 1;   // freeze
CardFrost.Amount = 0;   // thaw

Melt it around the pointer

MeltRadius is the radius of the clear patch around the pointer; 0 turns melting off. PointAt(at) melts the ice around a point from code, in the host's coordinates, for keyboard focus or a tour; PointAt(null) lets it freeze back:

PlayButton.GotFocus += (_, _) =>
{
    var center = new Point(PlayButton.Bounds.Width / 2, PlayButton.Bounds.Height / 2);
    CardFrost.PointAt(PlayButton.TranslatePoint(center, CardHost));
};
PlayButton.LostFocus += (_, _) => CardFrost.PointAt(null);

CrystalSize sets the size of the crystal pattern, Blur how frosted the content under the ice is, and Sparkle the glints.

Methods

Call PointAt to melt the ice without a pointer.

Method Description
PointAt(Point? at) Melts the ice around at (host coordinates) from code; null lets it freeze back.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Amount double 0.6 How far the ice has crept in from the edges; 1 covers it all. Range 0–1 (0–100%).
CrystalSize double 22 Size of the crystal pattern. Range 4–80 DIPs.
Blur double 6 How frosted: the blur of what is under the ice. Range 0.5–24 DIPs.
Tint Color #DCECFF The color the ice whitens what is under it toward; the crystals take it too.
MeltRadius double 60 Radius of the clear patch around the pointer; 0: no melting. Range 0–200 DIPs.
Sparkle double 0.5 Glints in the ice. Range 0–1 (0–100%).

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. Seed changes the crystal pattern; frost has no clock of its own, so Speed doesn't apply.

Notes

  • It frosts what is drawn before it. It copies what is under it just before it draws (see Layers that read the backdrop). Over the child, the default, that is the child and what shows through it. With Placement="Under" it frosts only what is behind the host, and the child stays sharp on top. Inside an offscreen layer (an ancestor with Opacity below 1, an effect, or a clip that needs a layer) the copy can't see that ancestor's content, so keep such ancestors opaque.
  • Light and dark surfaces: the layer knows the surface. On a light one the crystal lines darken a little, where white ice alone would vanish. Over a colorful picture in a light app they can all but vanish: set Surface="Dark" on the host, as the gallery does (see Light and dark surfaces).
  • Reduced motion: the melt jumps to the pointer instead of easing, and appears and fades at once.
  • Cost: about 0.63 ms of GPU time a frame on the benchmark card, including a blurred copy of what is under it. It doesn't animate, so it only redraws when what is under it changes or the melt moves. See Performance.
