Glass

Glass turns the host into a pane of liquid glass: a beveled rim that bends what is behind it and splits it into color, a slight magnification, an optional frost, a specular rim facing the light and a soft drop shadow. Use it for floating toolbars, a now-playing card, or buttons over a picture or a moving scene. It draws under the child and shows what is drawn behind the host, so it needs something behind it and a see-through child.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add a glass pane

Put the host over the content it should bend, here a photo in the same Grid, and give it a child without a background:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <Grid>
        <Image Source="/Assets/photo.jpg" Stretch="UniformToFill" />
        <fx:FXHost HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center" CornerRadius="22">
            <fx:FXHost.Layers>
                <fx:Glass />
            </fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <Border Width="210" Height="96">
                <TextBlock Text="Now playing" FontWeight="SemiBold"
                           HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center" />
            </Border>
        </fx:FXHost>
    </Grid>
</UserControl>

Give it something to bend

Glass copies the region of the window behind the host just before it draws (see Layers that read the backdrop). In practice:

  • Something must be behind it. It bends what was drawn there first: the page's background, earlier siblings in a Grid, a list scrolling under it. Over a flat color there is nothing to bend, and only the rim light and the shadow show. It is at its best over a picture, text or moving content, and follows anything that moves there.
  • Keep the child see-through. The glass draws under the child, so a Background on the child hides it. Put the label in a Border without a background, or a faintly translucent one.
  • Keep ancestors opaque. Inside an offscreen layer (an ancestor with Opacity below 1, an effect, or a clip that needs a layer) the copy can't see that ancestor's content.
  • Controls drawn after the host aren't bent. They sit in front of the glass.

Frost and tint it

Frost goes from clear (0) to frosted (1). Tint and TintAmount color the pane; a dark tint suits a dark theme:

<fx:Glass Frost="0.6" Tint="#1B1D22" TintAmount="0.3" />

LightAngle sets where the light that catches the rim comes from (-120 by default, above and to the left), and Rim how strong it is.

Bring the glass in and out

Presence makes the glass materialize by lensing: refraction, rim and shadow come in together. Animate it with a transition, here when a host with the toolbar class (<fx:FXHost x:Name="ToolbarHost" Classes="toolbar">) also gets shown:

<Style Selector="fx|FXHost.toolbar fx|Glass">
    <Setter Property="Presence" Value="0" />
    <Setter Property="Transitions">
        <Transitions>
            <DoubleTransition Property="Presence" Duration="0:0:0.4" />
        </Transitions>
    </Setter>
</Style>
<Style Selector="fx|FXHost.toolbar.shown fx|Glass">
    <Setter Property="Presence" Value="1" />
</Style>

ToolbarHost.Classes.Set("shown", true);

At a Presence of 0 the glass draws nothing and copies nothing.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Bevel double 14 Width of the beveled rim. Range 0.5–48 DIPs.
Depth double 16 How far the rim bends what is behind it, DIPs. Range 0–48.
Dispersion double 0.14 Color split at the rim. Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Magnification double 1.04 How much the glass enlarges what is behind it: 1 not at all, above 1 larger, below 1 smaller. Range 0.8–1.6.
Presence double 1 0..1: the glass materializes by lensing (refraction, rim and shadow together). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Frost double 0 0: clear; 1: frosted. Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Shadow double 0.35 How dark the soft drop shadow under the glass is (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Rim double 1.6 Strength of the rim light. Range 0–3.
LightAngle double -120 Where the light that catches the rim is, degrees: 0 to the right, -90 above; positive turns clockwise. Range -180 to 180.
Tint Color #FFFFFF A color the glass multiplies what is behind it by, as much as TintAmount says.
TintAmount double 0 How much Tint colors the glass (0: clear). Range 0–1 (0–100%).

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. Glass is still, so Speed and Seed don't apply.

Notes

  • It draws past the shape. The shadow falls about 5 DIPs down and fades out about 25 DIPs from the shape. A clipping ancestor cuts it: leave room around the host.
  • Light and dark surfaces: it draws the same on both. Pick a Tint per theme with a theme resource (see Light and dark surfaces).
  • Reduced motion: glass has no motion of its own, so nothing changes.
  • Cost: about 0.15–0.25 ms of GPU time a frame on the benchmark card, plus one copy of the region behind it. It doesn't animate: it redraws when what is behind it changes. See Performance.
