Pointer shadow

Pointer shadow treats the pointer as a light over the page: the content casts a soft shadow away from it, in the shape of its own pixels, further and softer the further the light is. Every card on a page with the layer casts its shadow away from the same pointer, which gives a flat layout a sense of depth. With no pointer nearby, the light rests above the content and the shadow falls just below it. The shadow is drawn under the content.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add a pointer shadow

Add the layer to a host around the card, and leave room around it for the shadow:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="12" Margin="48" HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:PointerShadow />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Border Width="240" Height="140" CornerRadius="12" Padding="16" Background="White">
            <TextBlock Text="Inbox" Foreground="#1f2937" FontWeight="SemiBold" />
        </Border>
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

Lift it higher

Elevation is how high the content floats over the page: higher casts a longer, softer shadow. LightHeight is how high the light is: lower casts longer shadows. MaxOffset caps how far the shadow moves from under the content, and Softness sets its blur right under it; it softens as it moves out. Darkness and Color set its tone.

<fx:PointerShadow Elevation="24" LightHeight="180" MaxOffset="40" Softness="10" Darkness="0.35" />

Reach and pace

Within PointerReach of the content (800 DIPs by default), the light follows the pointer across the window, even when it isn't over the host. FollowTime is how long the light takes to catch up with the pointer, and Fade how long it takes to move to the pointer when it arrives and back to rest when it leaves.

Move the light yourself

Set FollowPointer="False" and call PointAt(at) to place the light from code, in the host's coordinates; PointAt(null) sends it back to rest. For one light over several cards, translate the same point into each host:

// LightAt is a point in Stage, the panel that holds both cards.
ShadowA.PointAt(Stage.TranslatePoint(LightAt, CardHostA));
ShadowB.PointAt(Stage.TranslatePoint(LightAt, CardHostB));

With FollowPointer on, PointAt works too, and the pointer takes over when it moves within reach.

Methods

Call PointAt to place the light from code.

Method Description
PointAt(Point? at) Puts the light at at (host coordinates), as if the pointer were there. Null sends it back to rest. The pointer takes over when it moves within reach, unless FollowPointer is off.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Color Color #000000 The shadow's color.
Darkness double 0.3 How dark the shadow is (a dark page gets a deeper one, or it would not show). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Elevation double 14 How high the content floats over the page: higher casts a longer, softer shadow. Range 0–80 DIPs.
LightHeight double 240 How high the light is over the page: lower casts longer shadows. Range 60–1500 DIPs.
MaxOffset double 32 The furthest the shadow moves from under the content. Range 0–120 DIPs.
Softness double 8 The blur of the shadow right under the content; it softens as it moves out. Range 0–40 DIPs.
FollowPointer bool true Whether the light follows the pointer; off, it moves only with PointAt (a light of your own).
PointerReach double 800 How far from the content the pointer still moves the light. Range 0–3000 DIPs. Applies when FollowPointer is true.
FollowTime double 0.15 How long the light takes to catch up with the pointer (a time constant). Range 0–1 seconds.
Fade double 0.5 How long the light takes to move to the pointer when it arrives, and back to rest when it leaves. Range 0–2 seconds.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. The shadow follows the pointer, not a clock, so Speed and Seed do nothing.

Notes

  • The content's own shape. The shadow comes from the content's pixels, so transparent parts cast none and a round avatar casts a round shadow. The host captures the content; after changing something deep inside it, call RefreshMask() on the host. See Content masks.
  • Light and dark. A dark page gets a deeper shadow, or it wouldn't show. See Light and dark surfaces.
  • Room around it. The shadow reaches past the host by up to MaxOffset plus its blur. Leave room, or a clipping ancestor cuts it.
  • Reduced motion. The light jumps to the pointer instead of easing. See the poses.
  • Cost. About 0.16 ms of GPU time a frame on a card, since its shadow covers a large area. See What a layer costs.
