Starfield

Starfield paints a night sky: twinkling stars in three depths that drift slowly, the nearer ones faster. Its Meteors style adds shooting stars now and then, and Warp sends streaks rushing out from the center. Use it behind a hero, a splash or a launch screen, or as a quiet sky under other effects such as Fireworks and Trail. It's a fill: it paints the host's shape under the child.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add a starfield

Put the host around the panel, and leave the panel without a background so the sky shows through it.

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="16">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Starfield Style="Warp" />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Grid Height="260">
            <TextBlock Text="Launching…" Foreground="White" FontSize="18" FontWeight="SemiBold"
                       HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center" />
        </Grid>
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

Styles

Value Description
Stars (default) Twinkling stars drifting in three depths.
Meteors Stars, with shooting stars streaking across now and then.
Warp Streaks rushing out from the center, as at warp speed.

Add shooting stars

With Style="Meteors", shooting stars streak across the sky over the stars. MeteorRate is about how many fly each second, MeteorAngle the way they travel in degrees (0 to the right, 90 down; the default 145 runs down and to the left), MeteorLength the length of a tail in DIPs, and MeteorColor their color. They start along the top and the side they come from.

<fx:Starfield Style="Meteors" MeteorRate="1" MeteorAngle="35" MeteorLength="140" MeteorColor="#FFE9B0" />

Fit the sky to the shape

Density sets how many stars there are (or, in Warp, how many streaks), StarSize how big they are in DIPs, and Twinkle how much they twinkle, from 0 (steady) to 1. The stars are scattered in cells, and on a short shape the cells shrink to its height (down to 20 DIPs), so a strip such as a header or a button still holds stars in every depth.

<fx:Starfield Density="2" StarSize="1" Twinkle="0.9" />

Put the stars over your own sky

BackgroundColor is the sky behind the stars (#05060D by default); its alpha is how opaque it is. A transparent sky lets what is under the host show, such as a gradient on a border around it:

<Border CornerRadius="16">
    <Border.Background>
        <LinearGradientBrush StartPoint="0%,0%" EndPoint="0%,100%">
            <GradientStop Color="#0B1026" Offset="0" />
            <GradientStop Color="#3B1E5F" Offset="1" />
        </LinearGradientBrush>
    </Border.Background>
    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="16">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Starfield BackgroundColor="Transparent" Density="0.6" />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Grid Height="240" />
    </fx:FXHost>
</Border>

The stars are added as light, so keep what is under them dark.

Set the pace

Speed multiplies the clock: the drift, the twinkle, the shooting stars and the warp streaks. A slower sky makes a calm backdrop for other effects; the gallery puts this one under a pointer trail:

<fx:Starfield Density="0.6" Twinkle="0.4" Speed="0.3" />

Fills run on one shared clock, so starfields on different hosts, with the same settings, drift in step, whenever each host was created.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Style StarfieldStyle Stars What the sky shows: drifting stars, stars with shooting stars, or warp streaks. Values: Stars, Meteors, Warp.
StarColor Color #E8EEFF The color of the stars and of the warp streaks.
MeteorColor Color #BCD4FF The color of the shooting stars. Applies to the Meteors style.
BackgroundColor Color #05060D The sky behind the stars; transparent lets what is under the host show.
Density double 1 How many stars (in Warp, how many streaks), a multiple of the default. Range 0.1–3.
Twinkle double 0.6 How much the stars dim and brighten (0: steady). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
StarSize double 1.4 Size of the stars, in DIPs; the nearest are the largest. In Warp, the width of the streaks. Range 0.3–4.
MeteorAngle double 145 The way shooting stars travel, degrees: 0 to the right, 90 down; positive turns clockwise. Range -180 to 180. Applies to the Meteors style.
MeteorRate double 0.5 About how many shooting stars appear each second. Range 0.05–3 per second. Applies to the Meteors style.
MeteorLength double 90 Length of a shooting star's tail, in DIPs. Range 10–400. Applies to the Meteors style.
BorderWidth double 0 Width of a band inside the edge, DIPs. 0: the whole shape. Range 0–48.
UseMask bool false Paints only the child's own pixels (text, icons, a logo) instead of the shape: gradient text, a liquid-metal logo, holographic icons. It then goes over the child unless Placement says otherwise. The host captures the child to know its pixels: call RefreshMask after changing something deep inside it.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. On a fill, Intensity is its coverage, up to 1, and Seed picks where the stars are.

Notes

  • Light and dark surfaces: the starfield doesn't change with the surface. Its default sky is dark, so it looks the same in both themes. With a transparent BackgroundColor, the stars only add light, and they fade out over a light surface. See Light and dark surfaces.
  • Reduced motion: the stars hold still and stop twinkling, no shooting stars fly, and the warp streaks hold still. See Reduced motion.
  • Cost: about 0.11 ms of GPU time a frame on the benchmark's 340×190 card. The cost grows with the area. See Performance.
  • Bands and masks: like every fill, it has BorderWidth, which paints only a band inside the edge, and UseMask, which paints only the child's own pixels, such as large text. See Content masks.
