Dither

Retro, one-bit graphics: a tone (a ramp, a radial glow, drifting noise, or what is drawn behind the layer) is quantized into a few colors in square pixels, with an ordered pattern that turns shades into checkers and dots. Two colors give one-bit art; more give a small palette, like an old handheld's four greens. Use it for retro and game interfaces, posters and textured panels. It is a fill: it paints the host's shape and goes under the child.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add a dither

The fill goes under the child, so leave the child without a background:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="16">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Dither />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Border Width="340" Height="190" />
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

The default Bayer preset dithers a diagonal ramp into two colors.

Presets and inputs

Each preset brings a palette and sets Pattern, Input, PixelSize and Contrast. Set any of them yourself to adjust a preset. Input picks the tone that is dithered.

Preset

Value Description
Bayer (default) One-bit Bayer 4×4 over a diagonal ramp.
Handheld Four greens, Bayer 8×8, over drifting noise.
Grain One-bit blue noise over a radial ramp.
Backdrop One-bit Bayer 4×4 over what is drawn behind it.

Input

Value Description
Linear (default) A ramp across the shape along the angle.
Radial Light at the center, dark at the rim.
Noise Slowly drifting noise.
Backdrop What is drawn behind the layer, by luminance: re-tones the content under the shape.

Handheld drifts slowly in four greens. A label in the darkest green sits well on it:

<fx:FXHost CornerRadius="16">
    <fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <fx:Dither Preset="Handheld" />
    </fx:FXHost.Layers>
    <Border Width="120" Height="116" Padding="12">
        <TextBlock Text="Handheld" FontSize="13" FontWeight="SemiBold" Foreground="#0F380F"
                   VerticalAlignment="Bottom" />
    </Border>
</fx:FXHost>

Use your own colors

List the colors in Colors from dark to light, up to six. The darkest tones take the first color, the lightest the last:

<fx:Dither Colors="#1B1530, #6D597A, #E56B6F, #FFD7A8" />

Choose the pattern and the tone

Pattern sets how the shades break into pixels: Bayer2, Bayer4 and Bayer8 are ordered grids of 2×2, 4×4 and 8×8 thresholds, with more steps of shade as the grid grows; BlueNoise spreads the pixels evenly with no visible grid; WhiteNoise picks each pixel at random. PixelSize is the size of one dither pixel, in DIPs.

The tone comes from Input: a Linear ramp along RampAngle, a Radial one (light in the center, dark at the rim), or drifting Noise sized by NoiseScale and moving at FlowSpeed. Bias shifts the tone lighter or darker before it is quantized, and Contrast spreads it:

<fx:Dither Pattern="BlueNoise" Input="Noise" PixelSize="3" NoiseScale="140" Bias="0.1" />

Re-tone what is behind

With Input="Backdrop" (or Preset="Backdrop"), the dither reads what is drawn behind it and redraws it in its colors, like a one-bit screen. Under the child (the default) it re-tones what is behind the host; with Placement="Over", it re-tones the child as well:

<fx:FXHost CornerRadius="14">
    <fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <fx:Dither Preset="Backdrop" />
    </fx:FXHost.Layers>
    <Border Width="120" Height="120" />
</fx:FXHost>

Each dither pixel takes the average of what it covers. See Layers that read the backdrop for the limits.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Preset DitherPreset Bayer A ready-made look: sets Pattern, Input, PixelSize and Contrast, and the palette used while Colors is unset. Local values win. Values: Bayer, Handheld, Grain, Backdrop.
Colors FXColorList Unset The colors (up to six). Unset: the preset's palette.
Pattern DitherPattern Bayer4 The threshold pattern that spreads in-between tones over the pixels: an ordered Bayer grid, blue noise or white noise. Values: Bayer4, Bayer2, Bayer8, BlueNoise, WhiteNoise.
Input FXToneInput Linear The tone that is dithered: a linear or radial ramp, drifting noise, or what is drawn behind the layer. Values: Linear, Radial, Noise, Backdrop.
PixelSize double 2 Size of one dither pixel. Range 0.5–16 DIPs.
RampAngle double 34 Linear input: the way the ramp runs from dark to light, degrees: 0 left to right; positive turns clockwise. Range -180 to 180. Applies to the Linear input.
NoiseScale double 90 Noise input: feature size. Range 8–400 DIPs. Applies to the Noise input.
FlowSpeed double 0.2 Noise input: drift speed. Range 0–2 per second. Applies to the Noise input.
Bias double 0 Shifts the tone before quantizing. Range -1 to 1.
Contrast double 1 How far the tone is stretched around its middle before quantizing: above 1 pushes it toward the darkest and lightest colors, below 1 toward the middle ones. Range 0.2–4.
BorderWidth double 0 Width of a band inside the edge, DIPs. 0: the whole shape. Range 0–48.
UseMask bool false Paints only the child's own pixels (text, icons, a logo) instead of the shape: gradient text, a liquid-metal logo, holographic icons. It then goes over the child unless Placement says otherwise. The host captures the child to know its pixels: call RefreshMask after changing something deep inside it.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale.

Notes

  • Intensity is the fill's coverage, from 0 to 1. Seed and Speed only matter with the Noise input.
  • Still unless it is noise: only the Noise input moves. The others hold still and request no frames, unless what is behind a Backdrop dither changes.
  • Text on a dither is hardest to read over the middle of the ramp, where the pattern is busiest. Put labels over its darkest or lightest part.
  • Light and dark surfaces: the dither draws the same on both.
  • Reduced motion: the noise holds one pose. See Reduced motion.
  • Cost: 0.040 ms of GPU time per frame on a 340×190 card in the gallery's bench. On the Backdrop input it also copies what is behind it. Leave RenderScale at 1, or the pixels soften.
