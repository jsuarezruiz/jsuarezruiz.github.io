Inner shadow

InnerShadow puts light and shade inside the child's own shape, lit from LightAngle: pressed in (inset), raised (emboss), or pressed into the page with a fine highlight below (letterpress). Use it for an inset text field, debossed labels, raised icons or a soft, tactile look. It follows the child's pixels, not just its box: when the host captures the child, it measures how far each pixel is from the edge, so it works on glyphs as well as rounded fields.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add an inner shadow

An inset text field is the classic use. Wrap the field in a host:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost>
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:InnerShadow />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <TextBox PlaceholderText="Ask anything…" Width="360" Height="52" CornerRadius="26"
                 Padding="22,0" VerticalContentAlignment="Center" BorderThickness="0" />
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

Styles

Value Description
Inset (default) Pressed in: the walls on the light's side cast a shadow inward. On a dark surface the far walls also catch a faint lip of light, so the recess still reads.
Emboss Raised: the edges toward the light catch it, the far ones fall into shade.
Letterpress Pressed into the page: an inset shadow, and a fine highlight just below the far edges.

Press letters into the page

On text, the shading follows each glyph. Letterpress presses the letters in and adds a highlight just below their far edges; Emboss raises them. Here the pressed letters are a gray just darker than the page:

<Border Background="#DDE2E8" Padding="24" CornerRadius="12">
    <StackPanel Spacing="6">
        <fx:FXHost HorizontalAlignment="Center">
            <fx:FXHost.Layers>
                <fx:InnerShadow Style="Letterpress" Depth="2" Softness="1.5" Shadow="0.5" Highlight="0.9" />
            </fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <TextBlock Text="PRESSED" FontSize="40" FontWeight="Black" Foreground="#B8C0CB" />
        </fx:FXHost>
        <fx:FXHost HorizontalAlignment="Center">
            <fx:FXHost.Layers>
                <fx:InnerShadow Style="Emboss" Depth="2" Softness="2" Shadow="0.5" Highlight="0.7" />
            </fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <TextBlock Text="Raised" FontSize="30" FontWeight="Black" Foreground="#3B82F6" />
        </fx:FXHost>
    </StackPanel>
</Border>

Move the light

LightAngle is where the light comes from, in degrees: 0 from the right, -90 (the default) from above, positive turning clockwise. Depth is how far from an edge the shade reaches, and Softness how gradual it is:

<fx:InnerShadow LightAngle="-135" Depth="8" Softness="4" ShadowColor="#1E1B4B" Shadow="0.6" />

HighlightColor and Highlight apply to Emboss and Letterpress.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Style InnerShadowStyle Inset The look: pressed in (inset), raised (emboss), or pressed into the page (letterpress). Values: Inset, Emboss, Letterpress.
LightAngle double -90 Where the light comes from, degrees: 0 from the right, -90 from above; positive turns clockwise. Range -180 to 180.
Depth double 5 How deep the shape looks: how far from an edge the shade reaches, DIPs. Range 0.5–12.
Softness double 7 How soft the shade's edge is, in DIPs (0: crisp). Range 0–12.
ShadowColor Color #000000 The shade's color.
Shadow double 0.4 Strength of the shade (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
HighlightColor Color #FFFFFF The color of the lit edges. Applies to the Emboss and Letterpress styles.
Highlight double 0.6 Strength of the lit edges (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%). Applies to the Emboss and Letterpress styles.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. The inner shadow is still, so Speed and Seed don't apply.

Notes

  • It follows the content's pixels. See Content masks. The host watches only the child itself: after changing something deeper inside it, call RefreshMask.
  • A field is captured again as it changes. Typing changes its Text, and a hover or focus style changes its brushes. While changes keep coming, captures are spaced about 100 ms apart, and the distance field is rebuilt once they settle.
  • Depth is bounded. The distance field reaches 16 DIPs inside the edge, which bounds how far the shading reaches.
  • Light and dark surfaces: the layer knows the surface. On a dark one, an inset adds a faint lip of light along its far edges, so the recess still reads. See Light and dark surfaces.
  • Reduced motion: nothing moves, so nothing changes.
  • Cost: drawing is cheap (about 0.03 ms of GPU time a frame on the benchmark card) and happens once, since the layer doesn't animate. The cost is in the capture: building the distance field takes a few milliseconds on the UI thread each time the child changes, never per frame. See Content masks and Performance.
