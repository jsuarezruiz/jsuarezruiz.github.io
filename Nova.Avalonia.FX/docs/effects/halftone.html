Halftone

The look of print: round dots of ink on paper, laid on a rotated screen and sized by a tone, so dots grow and merge where the tone is dark and shrink to specks where it is light. The tone can be a ramp, a radial glow, drifting noise, or what is drawn behind the layer. Use it for comic and editorial styles, posters, retro buttons and textured cards. It is a fill: it paints the host's shape and goes under the child.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add a halftone

The fill goes under the child, so leave the child without a background:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost>
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Halftone />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Border Width="220" Height="56" CornerRadius="14">
            <TextBlock Text="Generate" FontWeight="SemiBold" Foreground="#1B1B1F"
                       HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center" />
        </Border>
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

By default the tone is radial: light in the middle, where a centered label sits, and dark at the rim.

Inputs

Input picks the tone the dots follow.

Value Description
Linear A ramp across the shape along the angle.
Radial (default) Light at the center, dark at the rim.
Noise Slowly drifting noise.
Backdrop What is drawn behind the layer, by luminance: re-tones the content under the shape.

Set the ink and the paper

InkColor is the dots' color and BackgroundColor the paper between them. DotPitch is the distance between dots, and ScreenAngle turns the screen; 0 puts the rows straight across, and the default is 45 degrees, as printed halftones are:

<fx:Halftone InkColor="#1D4ED8" BackgroundColor="#FEF3C7" DotPitch="8" ScreenAngle="15" />

Shape the tone

A Linear tone runs from dark to light along RampAngle; a Noise tone drifts, sized by NoiseScale and moving at FlowSpeed. Contrast spreads the tone, and Bias shifts it: a positive bias lightens it, which clears room for a label in the middle of a small button:

<fx:Halftone Input="Linear" RampAngle="90" Bias="0.45" Contrast="1.5" />

Re-tone what is behind

With Input="Backdrop", the dots follow the brightness of what is drawn behind the layer, each dot the average of its cell, like a newspaper photo. Under the child (the default) it re-tones what is behind the host; with Placement="Over", the child as well:

<fx:FXHost CornerRadius="14">
    <fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <fx:Halftone Input="Backdrop" InkColor="#111418" BackgroundColor="#F4EFE6" DotPitch="5" />
    </fx:FXHost.Layers>
    <Border Width="120" Height="120" />
</fx:FXHost>

See Layers that read the backdrop for the limits.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Input FXToneInput Radial The tone that sizes the dots: a linear or radial ramp, drifting noise, or what is drawn behind the layer. Values: Linear, Radial, Noise, Backdrop.
InkColor Color #1B1B1F The dots' color.
BackgroundColor Color #F1ECE1 The color between the dots.
DotPitch double 6 Distance between dots. Range 2–32 DIPs.
ScreenAngle double 45 The dot screen's rotation, degrees: 0 puts the rows straight across; positive turns it clockwise. Range -180 to 180.
RampAngle double 34 Linear input: the way the ramp runs from dark to light, degrees: 0 left to right; positive turns clockwise. Range -180 to 180. Applies to the Linear input.
NoiseScale double 90 Noise input: feature size. Range 8–400 DIPs. Applies to the Noise input.
FlowSpeed double 0.2 Noise input: drift speed. Range 0–2 per second. Applies to the Noise input.
Bias double 0 Shifts the tone before the dots are sized: positive lightens it (smaller dots), negative darkens it. Range -1 to 1.
Contrast double 1 How far the tone is stretched around its middle before the dots are sized: above 1 pushes dots toward empty or solid. Range 0.2–4.
BorderWidth double 0 Width of a band inside the edge, DIPs. 0: the whole shape. Range 0–48.
UseMask bool false Paints only the child's own pixels (text, icons, a logo) instead of the shape: gradient text, a liquid-metal logo, holographic icons. It then goes over the child unless Placement says otherwise. The host captures the child to know its pixels: call RefreshMask after changing something deep inside it.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale.

Notes

  • Intensity is the fill's coverage, from 0 to 1. Seed and Speed only matter with the Noise input.
  • Light and dark surfaces: on a dark surface the dots leave a margin of paper at the edge, so the shape's outline still shows. See Light and dark surfaces.
  • Text on a halftone: dots of either color can sit under a label, so give text a clear tone around it: ink-colored text where the tone is light, paper-colored text where it is dark.
  • Still unless it is noise: only the Noise input moves; the others hold still and request no frames. Under reduced motion the noise holds one pose.
  • Cost: 0.067 ms of GPU time per frame on a 340×190 card in the gallery's bench. On the Backdrop input it also copies what is behind it.
  • Like every fill, a halftone can paint only the child's own pixels with UseMask, or a band inside the edge with BorderWidth.
