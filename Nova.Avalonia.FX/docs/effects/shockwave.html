Shockwave

Shockwave sends out a ring of compressed air that bends everything it passes over as it spreads from a point, with a color split at its front, a highlight where there is content to catch it, and a faint rim so it still reads over a plain backdrop. Use it as press feedback on a key action, an impact in a game, or a moment when something lands. It is a one-shot: it fires on a press or from code, draws nothing between shots, and shots overlap. It draws over the child and reaches past the host, bending the host and what is around it.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add a shockwave

Wrap the control in a host. A press fires a ring from the press point (TriggerOnPress is on by default):

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="14" Cursor="Hand">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Shockwave Reach="150" />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Button Content="Press me" Width="150" Height="48" CornerRadius="14" />
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

Give it room and something to bend

The ring copies what is under it just before it draws (see Layers that read the backdrop). So:

  • It bends what is drawn before it: the child, and the page around the host out to Reach. Over text, edges and pictures the bend reads clearly; over a flat fill only the faint rim shows.
  • Leave room. The ring spreads up to Reach from its origin, plus a few widths of its front, well past the host. A clipping ancestor, such as a ScrollViewer or a Border with ClipToBounds, cuts it.
  • Controls drawn after the host cover the ring. Give the host a higher ZIndex when it should pass over its neighbors.
  • A press must land on the host's child. A child with no background in empty areas doesn't take presses: give it a Background, even Transparent.
  • Keep ancestors opaque. Inside an offscreen layer (an ancestor with Opacity below 1, an effect, or a clip that needs a layer) the copy can't see that ancestor's content.

Fire from code

Trigger(at) fires a ring from a point in the host's coordinates, or from the shape's center with no point. Set TriggerOnPress="False" to fire only from code:

private void OnTileReleased(object? sender, PointerReleasedEventArgs e)
{
    TileWave.Trigger(e.GetPosition(TileHost));
}

// From the center, when an upload finishes.
TileWave.Trigger();

Shape the ring

Reach is how far the ring spreads, Width how wide its front is, Displacement how far it bends what is behind it, Chroma the color split at the front (a share of Displacement) and Duration how long a shot lasts:

<fx:Shockwave Reach="240" Width="20" Displacement="14" Chroma="0.5" Duration="1.2" />

Methods

Call Trigger to fire a ring without a press. See One-shots.

Method Description
Trigger(Point? at = null) Fires a shockwave from at (host coordinates), or the shape's center.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Reach double 160 How far the ring spreads from where it starts, in DIPs. Range 16–600.
Width double 14 Width of the ring's front, in DIPs; it widens as it spreads. Range 2–60.
Displacement double 9 How far the front bends what is behind it. Range 0–40 DIPs.
Chroma double 0.35 Color split at the front, as a share of Displacement. Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Duration double 0.9 How long a ring takes to spread and fade, in seconds. Range 0.1–3.
TriggerOnPress bool true Fires a shockwave wherever the pointer is pressed on the host (on by default).

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. The ring has no randomness, so Seed doesn't apply.

Notes

  • Overlapping shots: firing again while a ring is still spreading starts another one beside it. When the layer's pool of live shots is full, the oldest fades out.
  • Light and dark surfaces: it draws the same on both.
  • Reduced motion: no travel: a press gives a soft brightening at its origin that cools.
  • Cost: about 0.26 ms of GPU time a frame on the benchmark card while a ring is live. Each frame shades only around the live rings, and between shots it draws nothing. See Performance.
