Caustics

Caustics paints the shifting web of light that water throws on the floor of a pool, over a water color, with an optional color split at the bright lines. Use it as a background for anything about water, summer or calm, or, with a transparent water color, to lay pool light over the content. It's a fill: by default it paints the host's shape under the child.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add caustics

Put the host around the panel, and leave the panel without a background so the water shows through it.

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="16">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:Caustics />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Grid Height="260">
            <TextBlock Text="Pool" Foreground="White" FontSize="28" FontWeight="Bold"
                       HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center" />
        </Grid>
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

Tune the web

Scale is the size of the web's cells in DIPs (70 by default), and Sharpness how thin the bright lines are. Brightness scales the light, LightColor tints it, and Dispersion splits it into colors at the bright lines (0 for none). WaterColor is the water under the light, and Depth darkens it toward the bottom, as if deeper (0 for none).

<fx:Caustics Scale="110" Sharpness="10" Brightness="1.2"
             Dispersion="0" Depth="0.2" WaterColor="#0E7490" />

Speed sets how fast the water moves. Fills run on one shared clock, so caustics on different hosts, with the same settings, shift in step.

Light the content with pool light

WaterColor's alpha is how opaque the water is. With a transparent water color, Placement="Over" and BlendMode="Screen", the layer lays only the light over the content, as if it sat at the bottom of a pool:

<fx:FXHost CornerRadius="16">
    <fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <fx:Caustics WaterColor="Transparent" Placement="Over" BlendMode="Screen" Brightness="0.8" />
    </fx:FXHost.Layers>
    <Image Source="/Assets/pool-tiles.jpg" Width="360" Height="240" Stretch="UniformToFill" />
</fx:FXHost>

Screen brightens what is beneath without passing white, so the light shows best on mid and dark tones; over white there is nothing left to brighten. See Blend modes.

Properties

Property Type Default Description
LightColor Color #D9FBFF The color of the web of light.
WaterColor Color #0B6E8F The water under the light; its alpha is how opaque it is.
Scale double 70 Size of the web's cells. Range 8–400 DIPs.
Brightness double 1 How bright the web of light is (0: only the water shows). Range 0–3.
Sharpness double 7 How thin the bright lines are. Range 1–20.
Dispersion double 0.3 Color split at the bright lines. Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Depth double 0.5 Darkening toward the bottom, as if deeper, as a share (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
BorderWidth double 0 Width of a band inside the edge, DIPs. 0: the whole shape. Range 0–48.
UseMask bool false Paints only the child's own pixels (text, icons, a logo) instead of the shape: gradient text, a liquid-metal logo, holographic icons. It then goes over the child unless Placement says otherwise. The host captures the child to know its pixels: call RefreshMask after changing something deep inside it.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. On a fill, Intensity is its coverage, up to 1.

Notes

  • Light and dark surfaces: with an opaque water color, the layer paints its own colors and looks the same on a light or a dark surface. See Light and dark surfaces.
  • Reduced motion: the web holds one fixed pose. See Reduced motion.
  • Cost: about 0.43 ms of GPU time a frame on the benchmark's 340×190 card, among the heavier backgrounds. With Dispersion above 0 the shader works the web out three times a pixel, once per color; at 0 it works it out once. The cost grows with the area: on a large panel, try RenderScale="0.5", which softens the lines a little. See Performance.
  • Bands and masks: like every fill, it has BorderWidth, which paints only a band inside the edge, and UseMask, which paints only the child's own pixels, such as large text. See Content masks.
