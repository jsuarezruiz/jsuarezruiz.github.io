Old film

OldFilm shows the content as an old print running through a projector: toned toward sepia, darker at the corners and grained, weaving a little in the gate and flickering at 18 frames a second, with scratches that run down it for a few frames and specks of dust that come and go. Use it for a "memories" card, a throwback hero, a retro photo viewer or a silent-movie title. It draws over the child and works on what is drawn there: the child, and whatever shows through it.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add old film

Wrap the content in a host. A photo, with tones from deep shadow to bright highlights, shows it best; a gradient works too:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="14">
        <fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <fx:OldFilm />
        </fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <Border Width="236" Height="128" CornerRadius="14">
            <Border.Background>
                <LinearGradientBrush StartPoint="0%,0%" EndPoint="100%,100%">
                    <GradientStop Color="#0EA5E9" Offset="0" />
                    <GradientStop Color="#22C55E" Offset="0.5" />
                    <GradientStop Color="#FACC15" Offset="1" />
                </LinearGradientBrush>
            </Border.Background>
            <StackPanel HorizontalAlignment="Center" VerticalAlignment="Center">
                <TextBlock Text="The Lodger" Foreground="White" FontWeight="Bold" FontSize="22" HorizontalAlignment="Center" />
                <TextBlock Text="1927" Foreground="White" FontSize="13" HorizontalAlignment="Center" />
            </StackPanel>
        </Border>
    </fx:FXHost>
</UserControl>

Give it a picture

The film copies what is under it just before it draws (see Layers that read the backdrop): the child, and whatever shows through it. With a see-through child, it shows what is behind the host as film. Either way, something must be drawn there first: controls drawn after the host sit in front of it. Inside an offscreen layer (an ancestor with Opacity below 1, an effect, or a clip that needs a layer) the copy can't see that ancestor's content, so keep such ancestors opaque.

Tone the print

Tint is the color the picture is toned toward, and Sepia how much: at 0 the picture keeps its own colors under the grain, flicker and scratches. A cooler tint gives a cyanotype:

<fx:OldFilm Tint="#6B8FC9" Sepia="0.8" />

Wear and tear

Grain, Flicker, Scratches and Dust run from 0 (none) to 1; Vignette darkens the corners and Weave is how far the picture moves in the gate. A lightly worn print:

<fx:OldFilm Grain="0.3" Flicker="0.2" Scratches="0.15" Dust="0.2" Weave="0.5" />

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Tint Color #C9A36B The color the picture is toned toward.
Sepia double 1 How far the picture is toned toward Tint, keeping its lightness (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Vignette double 0.6 How much the corners darken (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Grain double 0.5 How strong the film grain is (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Flicker double 0.5 How much each frame's brightness jumps (0: steady). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Scratches double 0.5 How often scratches run down the picture (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Dust double 0.5 How many specks of dust come and go (0: none). Range 0–1 (0–100%).
Weave double 1.2 How far the picture weaves in the gate. Range 0–6 DIPs.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale. Seed changes where the scratches and dust fall.

Notes

  • The projector keeps its own pace. The weave, flicker, scratches, dust and grain step at 18 frames a second, whatever the display runs at.
  • Light and dark surfaces: it draws the same on both.
  • Reduced motion: it holds one frame.
  • Cost: about 0.17 ms of GPU time a frame on the benchmark card. It animates, so it redraws while it is visible. See Performance.
