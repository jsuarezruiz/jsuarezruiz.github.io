Aurora

Northern lights inside the shape: three curtains of colored light drift slowly across it, each with a bright lower hem and fine rays, fading in from the shape's edge. Use it on hero banners, sign-in screens, empty states or a night-themed card. It draws under the host's child, as light, so it needs something dark behind it to glow against.

  • Light
  • Dark

Add an aurora

The aurora goes under the child, so a background on the child would hide it. Put the dark sky outside the host instead, and leave the child see-through:

<UserControl xmlns="https://github.com/avaloniaui"
             xmlns:x="http://schemas.microsoft.com/winfx/2006/xaml"
             xmlns:fx="https://github.com/jsuarezruiz/Nova.Avalonia.FX">

    <Border CornerRadius="14" Background="#0B0F1A">
        <fx:FXHost CornerRadius="14">
            <fx:FXHost.Layers>
                <fx:Aurora Feather="22" Intensity="1.6" Scale="90" />
            </fx:FXHost.Layers>
            <Border Width="290" Height="128" Padding="16">
                <TextBlock Text="Northern lights" FontSize="17" FontWeight="Bold"
                           Foreground="White" VerticalAlignment="Bottom" />
            </Border>
        </fx:FXHost>
    </Border>
</UserControl>

The aurora draws the same in light and dark themes, so the same dark card works in both.

Set the colors

Each curtain has its own color: Color for the first, SecondColor and ThirdColor for the others. The defaults are green, blue and violet:

<fx:Aurora Color="#F472B6" SecondColor="#FB923C" ThirdColor="#A78BFA" />

Shape the curtains

Scale sets the size of the curtains' folds, and FlowSpeed how fast they drift; at 0 they hold still and the layer requests no more frames. Feather is how far inside the shape's edge the light fades in. Past a quarter of the shape's shorter side it counts only a quarter, so a small shape keeps a curtain rather than a thin streak:

<fx:Aurora Scale="200" FlowSpeed="0.1" Feather="60" />

Fill a window

The aurora's cost grows with its area. For a window-sized background, shade it at half resolution. Soft, large layers like this one are what RenderScale is for:

<fx:Aurora RenderScale="0.5" />

Properties

Property Type Default Description
Color Color #16C79A The top curtain's color.
SecondColor Color #3B6CFF The middle curtain's color.
ThirdColor Color #A23BFF The bottom curtain's color.
Scale double 120 Size of the curtains' folds. Range 20–400 DIPs.
FlowSpeed double 0.25 How fast the curtains drift and ripple (0: still). Range 0–2 per second.
Feather double 40 How far inside the shape's edge the light fades in. Past a quarter of the shorter side it counts only a quarter, so a small shape keeps a curtain rather than a thin streak. Range 0–120 DIPs.

Every layer also has the common properties: Intensity, Opacity, Speed, Seed, IsActive, Placement, BlendMode and RenderScale.

Notes

  • Intensity is the strength of the light, up to 3. Seed changes the curtains' shapes.
  • Light and dark surfaces: the aurora doesn't adapt to the surface; it is light, and it reads best over a dark background placed behind the host. For pure additive light, set BlendMode="Screen" or "Plus" (see Blend modes).
  • Reduced motion: the curtains hold one pose. See Reduced motion.
  • Cost: 0.127 ms of GPU time per frame on a 340×190 card in the gallery's bench. On a 700×500 panel it takes about 0.62 ms, and 0.28 ms with RenderScale="0.5". See Render scale.
