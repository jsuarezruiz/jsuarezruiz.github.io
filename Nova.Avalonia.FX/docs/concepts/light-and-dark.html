Light and dark surfaces

Light layers such as a glow, a pulse, sparkles or lightning add light, as rune draws them. That reads well on a dark surface. On a light surface, added light would vanish into white, so these layers know which surface they sit on and change how they draw.

How light layers draw on each surface

On a dark surface, light adds to what is beneath it: rings, halos and stars brighten the background, and the hottest parts turn white.

On a light surface, glow, pulse, sparkles and lightning draw as tinted haze instead: saturated color, white-hot cores in the layer's own color, and faint light kept faint, so a radar's rings stay rings instead of merging into a wash. Lightning's core becomes a crisp line in a deep shade of the color over a pale tint of its glow. A white light, such as the spotlight's default, stays white. Very pale colors still read weakly on white, so pick deeper colors for light themes.

Other layers adjust to the surface in their own way:

Layer On a light surface On a dark surface
Fire Simulated flames draw as color over the smoke; the procedural smoke veil and dim tips stay fainter. Flames add light.
Fireworks The sparks show as color. The sparks add light.
Holo foil Pastel bands. A thin film's cooler colors, added as light.
Frost The crystal lines darken a little, where white ice alone would vanish. White ice.
Halftone The dots leave a margin of paper at the edge, so the outline still shows.
Inner shadow An inset adds a faint lip of light along its far edges.
Liquid progress The wave behind the surface is paler and see-through.
Pointer shadow A deeper shadow.
Reveal A dissolve's edge catches a faint light.
Shatter The shards' edges are dark.
Voice orb Its glassy rim is fainter.

The layers that know the surface are ambient glow, beam, border beam, electric, fire, fireworks, frost, glow, halftone, holo foil, ink, inner shadow, light leaks, light rays, liquid progress, pointer shadow, pulse, reveal, shatter, sparkles, spotlight, trail, voice orb and voice wave. The other layers draw the same on both.

How the host picks its surface

FXHost.Surface sets the surface for every layer in the host:

Surface Meaning
Auto (the default) Follows the host's theme: a light theme is a light surface, a dark theme a dark one.
Dark Light adds, as rune draws it.
Light Light shows as tinted haze, so it neither vanishes into white nor washes it out.

With Auto, the host reads its actual theme variant, so it follows the application's theme, a ThemeVariantScope around it, or a theme switch while the app runs. FXHost.IsLightSurface tells you which surface the host is using now.

Set the surface yourself when the theme doesn't describe what is behind the layers, for example a dark card in a light app:

<fx:FXHost Surface="Dark">
    <fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <fx:Pulse Style="Radar" Color="#22d3ee" />
    </fx:FXHost.Layers>
    <Border Width="64" Height="64" CornerRadius="32" Background="#101217" />
</fx:FXHost>

You can also put the card in a ThemeVariantScope with RequestedThemeVariant="Dark"; the host then picks the dark surface on its own, and so do the card's own theme resources.

Theme the colors

Layer colors are ordinary Color properties, so they can come from theme resources. Define a color per theme and use it with DynamicResource; the layer changes color when the theme changes:

<Application.Resources>
    <ResourceDictionary>
        <ResourceDictionary.ThemeDictionaries>
            <ResourceDictionary x:Key="Light">
                <Color x:Key="StatusGlowColor">#0e7490</Color>
            </ResourceDictionary>
            <ResourceDictionary x:Key="Dark">
                <Color x:Key="StatusGlowColor">#22d3ee</Color>
            </ResourceDictionary>
        </ResourceDictionary.ThemeDictionaries>
    </ResourceDictionary>
</Application.Resources>

<fx:FXHost CornerRadius="999">
    <fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <fx:Pulse Style="Radar" Color="{DynamicResource StatusGlowColor}" />
        <fx:Glow Color="{DynamicResource StatusGlowColor}" Radius="9" />
    </fx:FXHost.Layers>
    <Ellipse Width="14" Height="14" Fill="#16a34a" />
</fx:FXHost>

A few tips:

  • Fire reads best on dark UI.
  • For pure additive light on any surface, set BlendMode="Plus" or "Screen". See Blend modes.
  • Most fills, glass layers and backgrounds draw the same on both surfaces: give them colors that suit each theme, as above.
