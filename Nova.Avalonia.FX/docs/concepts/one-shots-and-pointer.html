One-shots and the pointer

Most layers run on their own. Some play a moment when asked (a ripple, a burst, a zap), some show and hide the content with a transition, and some follow the pointer. This page covers all three.

One-shots

A one-shot plays once and then draws nothing, so it costs nothing until it fires. Shots overlap: firing again while one is still playing starts another one beside it, so a quick double tap shows two ripples. Each layer keeps a bounded pool of live shots. When it is full, the pulse, the shockwave, the electric zaps and the sparkle bursts fade their oldest shot out; the other layers drop it. Ink and water add each splash to their simulation instead.

Layer From code On a press Notes
Pulse, Ripple style Trigger(at) TriggerOnPress (on) A ripple from a point, confined to the shape.
Pulse, Ping style Ping(to), PingTo(control) TriggerOnPress (on) A ping flies from the shape's edge to a point or a control. Interval sends one every so many seconds.
Sparkles Burst() BurstOnPress (off) Pops every star once. With IsContinuous off, stars only show during a burst.
Electric Zap(at) ZapOnPress (off) One strike. On an arc, a zap at a point strikes from the nearer node to it.
Shockwave Trigger(at) TriggerOnPress (on) A ring that bends what is behind it.
Glitch Trigger() TriggerOnPress (on) A short burst over the whole content. With IsContinuous it glitches now and then on its own instead.
Confetti Fire(at) FireOnPress (off) A burst from a point, or the style's own origin.
Fireworks Fire(at) FireOnPress (off) A rocket that bursts at a point. Interval keeps a show going.
Ink Splash(at) SplashOnPress (on) A splash of every color.
Water Splash(at) SplashOnPress (on) A splash on the water's surface.
DotGrid Ripple(at) RippleOnPress (on) A ring sent out across the grid.
Jelly Wobble(at) WobbleOnPress (on) The element squashes and wobbles.
Beam Send() Sends one pulse along the path. Pulses also leave every Interval seconds.

The point is in the host's coordinates. Leave it out (or pass null) to use the center of the shape or the layer's own spot. A press on the host passes the press point, except for a ping, which flies along its Direction.

To fire at a point from an event, convert the position to the host:

private void OnCardReleased(object? sender, PointerReleasedEventArgs e)
{
    CardRipple.Trigger(e.GetPosition(CardHost));
}

To send a ping from one control to another, give the sending host a higher ZIndex so the ping lands on top of the target:

<fx:FXHost ZIndex="1">
    <fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <fx:Pulse x:Name="Pinger" Style="Ping" TriggerOnPress="False" />
    </fx:FXHost.Layers>
    <Button Content="Send" Click="OnSend" />
</fx:FXHost>

<Button x:Name="Inbox" Content="Inbox" />

private void OnSend(object? sender, RoutedEventArgs e)
{
    Pinger.PingTo(Inbox);
}

Show and hide transitions

Reveal, Genie and Shatter show and hide the host's content. Set IsShown, which binds both ways by default, or call Show(from) and Hide(toward).

When a transition starts, the layer captures the content as it looks, hides the real element and plays the transition on the capture. Once the content is shown again, the element takes over in the same frame as the layer stops drawing. While hidden, the element is invisible but keeps its place in the layout. With nothing to capture (no content, invisible, or not laid out yet), or while the layer isn't active, it switches at once.

Duration sets the pace in seconds; the layer's Speed doesn't apply.

<fx:FXHost>
    <fx:FXHost.Layers>
        <fx:Reveal x:Name="DetailsReveal" Style="Iris" IsShown="{Binding IsDetailsVisible}" />
    </fx:FXHost.Layers>
    <Border Width="260" Height="140" CornerRadius="12" Background="#15171c" />
</fx:FXHost>

The point given to Show or Hide is where the transition comes from or goes: an iris opens there or closes on it, shards break from it and fly back toward it, the genie pours out of it or pulls into it.

private void OnDismiss(object? sender, PointerReleasedEventArgs e)
{
    DetailsReveal.Hide(e.GetPosition(DetailsHost));
}

Genie can also pull the content into another control, such as a dock icon or a trash button, with Target:

<fx:Genie Target="{Binding #TrashButton}" Duration="0.6" />

Other layers that change state with a short animation:

  • Spoiler hides content until it is tapped. IsRevealed binds both ways, Reveal(from) and Hide() work from code, and HideAfter covers it again after so many seconds.
  • PagePeel curls a corner up when hovered; IsPeeled holds it peeled from code.
  • LiquidProgress eases to a new Level with a little overshoot and sloshes its surface.
  • Glow cools over Cool seconds when IsLit turns off.
  • Frost creeps in as far as Amount; animate it with a transition to freeze or thaw.

Layers that follow the pointer

These layers react to the pointer moving over the host:

Layer What follows the pointer From code Reach
Spotlight A soft light that lights the border near it. PointAt(at) PointerReach, 48 DIPs past the shape by default
HoloFoil The sheen shifts as if the foil tilted. PointAt(at) The shape
Magnifier A glass loupe. PointAt(at) The shape
Trail A ribbon or sparks behind the pointer. PointAt(at) PointerReach, 0 by default
Ink The pointer stirs the fluid and leaves ink. PointAt(at) The shape
Water The pointer drags ripples across the surface. Splash(at) The shape
Frost The ice melts around the pointer. PointAt(at) The shape, while MeltRadius is above 0
DotGrid Dots light up and grow around the pointer. PointAt(at) HoverRadius, 90 DIPs by default
ParticleNetwork The pointer joins in and draws the dots toward it. PointAt(at) The shape, while FollowPointer is on
Tilt The host leans toward the pointer, with a glare. PointAt(at) PointerReach, 0 by default
PointerShadow The content casts a shadow away from the pointer. PointAt(at) PointerReach, 800 DIPs by default, while FollowPointer is on
PagePeel The corner peels further as the pointer nears it. IsPeeled The shape, while FollowPointer is on

The host listens to the window's pointer only while one of its layers follows it and is active. It passes the pointer's position to a layer while the pointer is within the shape plus the layer's reach, and tells the layer once the pointer leaves. On the render thread, the layer eases toward the pointer and fades in and out; where a layer has FollowTime, that is how long it takes to catch up. Idle, these layers draw nothing.

PointAt(at) aims a layer from code, as if the pointer were there: for keyboard focus, a guided tour or a demo. Pass null to let it go. The pointer takes over again when it moves.

This lights a card's spotlight on its button when the button gets keyboard focus:

SaveButton.GotFocus += (_, _) =>
{
    var center = new Point(SaveButton.Bounds.Width / 2, SaveButton.Bounds.Height / 2);
    CardSpotlight.PointAt(SaveButton.TranslatePoint(center, CardHost));
};
SaveButton.LostFocus += (_, _) => CardSpotlight.PointAt(null);
